# Chapter 3 plan: Minimisation

Internal planning file (the build skips it). It follows `_style.md`: the full arc for every
concept (intuition → definition → why → how → what goes wrong), at least 4 step-format worked
examples per concept (easy → medium → GATE → edge case; the edge case uses `level="gate"` and a
title starting "Edge case:"), a `:::derivation` or `:::theorem` + `:::proof` for every rule GATE
uses, animation wherever motion teaches, and every block carrying an `#id` and a `depth`.

- Folder: `content/gate-notes/dl/03-minimisation/`
- `pyq_topics`: `dl/minimization` (all four lessons), `dl/gates` (lesson 4, for `cse-2019-cs-50`).
- Check scripts (`content/gate-notes/_checks/dl/`): a shared `minlib.py` (cubes, the
  Quine–McCluskey tabulation, a brute-force prime-implicant scan over all 3ⁿ cubes that must agree
  with it, EPI / redundant / selective classification, Petrick's method cross-checked against an
  exhaustive subset search, minimal SOP and POS by literal and by term count, K-map geometry with
  wrap-around loop rectangles, static-hazard detection, multi-output sharing search, minimum NAND/NOR
  counts with complemented inputs available, VEM expansion) built on Chapter 2's `boollib.py`
  (expression parser, truth tables). One module per lesson, `l3_01_kmaps.py` … `l3_04_two_level.py`:
  every worked value, viz caption, PYQ key, practice answer and MSQ option is recomputed by
  enumeration; sympy's `simplify_logic` is an independent cross-check of minimal forms.
  `lessons_ch3.py` enforces the house structure, `pyq_ch3.py` the teacher-style solutions (and that
  every K-map animation in a solution plots the function the question defines), `viz_ch3.py` that
  the scenes equal their generator, `l3_lab_vectors.py` that the Live Lab agrees with `minlib`.
- Viz: our own scenes, generated by `_viz_ch3.py` from `minlib`, so no cell, loop or caption can
  drift. K-maps are Viz `grid` elements with Gray-order `text` labels; loops are `box` elements
  revealed one step at a time; a wrap-around loop is drawn as the 2 or 4 pieces `minlib.loop_rects`
  returns, each touching the map edge. Every scene has a phone (`narrow`) canvas.
- Sources: our own text. The sets (internal labels A–E) are a checklist only; facts were checked
  against Mano, *Digital Design* (gate-level minimisation, don't-cares, NAND/NOR implementation),
  McCluskey (1956) for the tabular method, Petrick (1956) for the covering expansion, and Unger /
  Mano for static hazards. Errata touching this chapter (`_errata/dl.md`): B 113 (the minimal SOP is
  not always unique), C 238 (an unmerged 5-variable pair), set C's "9 minimal SOPs" counting caveat.

## Lesson 1 · `dl-03-01-kmaps` · Karnaugh maps (2–4 variables)

**Prerequisites:** dl-02-04 (minterms), dl-01-08 (Gray code). **Covered by sets:** A 37–39; B 113–116;
C 232–238, 259–260, 263–264.

### Teaching sequence
1. **Radar** `#radar`; **intuition** `#why-maps` (the combining rule $xy + xy' = x$ is easy to apply
   only when the two rows sit side by side; a K-map is a truth table folded so that they always do).
2. **The combining theorem** `#combining` (theorem + proof; dual $(x + y)(x + y') = x$).
3. **Gray order and layout** `#gray-order`, `#map-layout` (2, 3, 4 variables; rows take the first
   variables); cell numbers `#cell-numbers` with `:::trick` `#trick-cell-numbers` (0 1 3 2 / 4 5 7 6 /
   12 13 15 14 / 8 9 11 10); animated `kmap-gray-layout`; `:::trap` `#trap-binary-labels`.
4. **Adjacency** `#adjacency` (theorem: map neighbours, wrap-around included, are exactly the minterms
   one bit away; proof from the Gray code's one-bit steps and its cyclic end); wrap-around and the
   torus `#wrap-around`; corners `#corner-quad`; animated `kmap-adjacency`, `kmap-corner-quad`.
5. **Groups** `#groups-def` (rectangles of 2ᵏ cells, wrapping allowed); theorem `#group-term` (a group
   of 2ᵏ cells is a product of n − k literals) + proof; reading a group `#read-group`; `:::trick`
   `#trick-read-group` (keep the variables that never change).
6. **Grouping rules and the SOP method** `#grouping-rules`, `#sop-method` (loners first, biggest legal
   group for each, stop when every 1 is covered); animated `kmap-grouping` (octet + two wrap quads).
   `:::trap` `#trap-greedy` (the redundant centre quad).
7. **POS from the 0s** `#pos-method`; theorem `#pos-duality` + proof; animated `kmap-pos-zeros`;
   `:::trick` `#trick-pos-labels` (0 → plain, 1 → complemented); `:::trap` `#trap-pos-literals`.
8. **Reading a given map** `#read-map` (which variables label the axes, which is the MSB);
   `:::trap` `#trap-axis-order`; `:::trap` `#trap-non-gray` with animated `kmap-trap-labels`.
9. **Patterns** `#xor-patterns` (checkerboard = parity; partial checkerboards $BD + B'D'$); theorem
   `#checkerboard` + proof; animated `kmap-checkerboard`.
10. **Free variables** `#free-variable` (a variable is free iff flipping it maps the 1s onto the 1s);
    `:::trick` `#trick-free-variable`.
11. **Algebra alongside** `#algebra-check`; **equivalent forms** `#equivalent-forms` (compare minterm
    lists, not shapes); **counting minterms** `#count-minterms`; **functions given by conditions**
    `#partial-specs` (fill the map region by region; cells no condition reaches are free).
12. **Live Lab** `#lab-kmap` (K-map Solver, map tab). GATE questions, practice, summary `#summary-card`.

### Derivations and proofs
- `:::theorem` combining rule + proof; `:::theorem` map adjacency = one-bit adjacency + proof;
  `:::theorem` a 2ᵏ group is an (n − k)-literal product + proof; `:::theorem` POS of f = complement of
  the SOP of f′ + proof; `:::theorem` the checkerboard is parity + proof.

### Worked examples (4 per concept)
- **Map layout and adjacency:** place Σ(1, 4, 6) on a 3-variable map (easy); neighbours of m₁₃ (medium);
  is m₀ adjacent to m₈ and m₂ (GATE); edge case: a map drawn with binary-order labels.
- **SOP by grouping:** Σ(0, 2, 3, 6, 7) over (A, B, C) (easy); Σ(0, 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12, 13, 14) (medium);
  Σ(0, 2, 4, 8, 10, 11, 12), the 2025 pattern (GATE); edge case: Σ(1, 5, 6, 7, 11, 12, 13, 15), the
  redundant centre quad.
- **POS:** Π over 3 variables (easy); a 4-variable POS (medium); the 2011 POS → SOP (GATE); edge case:
  a function whose POS is cheaper than its SOP (the 2014 function).
- **Reading maps and patterns:** a map with rows CD and columns AB (easy); the binary-labelled 1998 map
  (medium); a checkerboard (GATE); edge case: a map whose labels use barred letters.
- **Free variables and equivalence:** is Σ(1, 3, 4, 6, 9, 11, 12, 14) independent of a variable (easy);
  which SOPs are equal to a function (medium); count the minterms of a complemented SOP (GATE);
  edge case: a function fixed by two conditions with two cells left free (the GATE 2021 pattern uses three).

### Tricks and traps
- `:::trick` `#trick-cell-numbers`, `#trick-read-group`, `#trick-pos-labels`, `#trick-free-variable`.
- `:::trap` `#trap-binary-labels`, `#trap-greedy`, `#trap-pos-literals`, `#trap-axis-order`,

## Lesson 2 · `dl-03-02-prime-implicants` · Implicants, prime implicants and don't-cares

### Worked examples (4 per concept)
