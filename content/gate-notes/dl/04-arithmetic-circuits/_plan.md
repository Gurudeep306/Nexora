Internal planning file (the build skips it). It follows `_style.md`: the full arc for every
concept (intuition → definition → why → how → what goes wrong), at least 4 step-format worked
examples per concept (easy → medium → GATE → edge case; the edge case uses `level="gate"` and a
title starting "Edge case:"), a `:::derivation` or `:::theorem` + `:::proof` for every rule GATE
uses, a recap note at the end of each section, animation wherever motion teaches, and every block
carrying an `#id` and a `depth`. Lessons are written to Chapter 5 depth.

- Folder: `content/gate-notes/dl/04-arithmetic-circuits/`, `chapter: arithmetic-circuits`.
- `pyq_topics`: `dl/arithmetic` and `dl/combinational` (lessons 1–3), `dl/arithmetic` (lesson 4).
- **Hardware only.** Every number-level rule is already taught in Chapter 1:
  [Lesson 9](lesson:code-arithmetic) (binary addition, complements, overflow and its carry rule,
  carry vs overflow flags, BCD +6 and excess-3 corrections) and
  [Lesson 10](lesson:multiplication-booth) (shift-and-add, signed products, Booth and bit-pair
  recoding). These lessons link back to those blocks (`#overflow-theorem`, `#carry-vs-overflow`,
  `#bcd-add`, `#bcd-why-six`, `#binary-mul`, `#booth-recode`, `#booth-algorithm`, `#booth-count`)
  and never re-derive them; they build the circuits on top.
- The chapter comes before the combinational building blocks (Chapter 5, since the 2026-10-19
  reorder). The carry-select adder needs a 2:1 MUX, so it uses one as "a switch that passes one of two
  inputs" with a forward link to [Chapter 5, Lesson 2](lesson:multiplexers).
- Check scripts (`content/gate-notes/_checks/dl/`): a shared `arithlib.py` (gate netlists in
  comblib's format for half/full adders and subtractors (two constructions), ripple-carry,
  multi-level carry look-ahead with fan-in-k blocks, carry-select, the adder–subtractor with C, V, N,
  Z flags, the BCD adder, the 1996 adder converter, the 2003 ALU, the array multiplier, sequential
  and serial adders, the 2002 pipelined adder; static arrival times with a delay per gate type and a
  transport-delay simulation that gives each line's settling time for one input change). Every
  netlist is checked by exhaustive (or, above 8 bits, 3000-sample random) simulation against
  integer arithmetic. One module per lesson, `l4_01_ripple.py` … `l4_04_multipliers.py`:
  every worked value, caption number, PYQ key, practice answer and MSQ option is recomputed.
  `lessons_ch4.py` enforces the house structure, `pyq_ch4.py` the teacher-style solutions,
  `viz_ch4.py` that the scenes equal their generator, `l4_lab_vectors.py` that the Live Lab agrees
  with `arithlib`.
- Viz: our own scenes generated by `_viz_ch4.py` (it reuses the drawing, merging and layout-check
  machinery of `_viz_ch5.py`), so no carry, delay or sum can drift. Gates are Viz `gate` elements,
  adders and registers are `box` elements, wires are `line` elements whose tone shows the logic level
  (success = 1, muted = 0, accent = the active carry or critical path). Every scene has a phone
  (`narrow`) canvas and a caption on every step (the captions are the text alternative).
- **Delay conventions.** GATE questions use several delay models; each lesson states them in one
  table (`#delay-models`) and every solution names the model its question fixes: (a) **unit gate
  delay**, full adder from two half adders + OR: carry out after $2n + 1$, last sum after $2n$;
  (b) **two-level SOP full adder** with free complements: $2n$; (c) **block model**: each full adder
  is one box of delay $t_{FA}$, total $n\,t_{FA}$; (d) **carry/sum split**: $(n-1)t_c + \max(t_s, t_c)$.
  CLA: p/g one level (or two when XOR is built as AND-OR), every lookahead level two, the sum XOR one
  (or two).
- Sources: our own text. The sets (internal labels A–E) are a checklist only; facts were checked
  against Mano, *Digital Design* (half/full adders and subtractors, ripple and look-ahead adders,
  the BCD adder, the adder–subtractor) and Mano, *Computer System Architecture* and Hamacher et al.,
  *Computer Organization* (array multipliers and their delay, sequential and Booth multiplier
  hardware, 74181/74182 hierarchies).

a key or a figure looked doubtful):**
- All 14 keys agree with a from-scratch derivation under the delay convention each question states.
- `cse-2015-2-cs-48` (NAT 19.2, `needsReview` in the bank): the question gives the full adder's
  internals and then says the 4-bit adder "is implemented by using four full adders". With XOR = 2.4 µs
  and AND/OR = 1.2 µs a full adder's sum and carry both take 4.8 µs; treating each full adder as a block
  of 4.8 µs gives 4 × 4.8 = **19.2** µs, the official key (an exam archive lists 19.2 as the
  correct answer). A gate-level critical path (the carry in reaches the carry out through only an AND
  and an OR, 2.4 µs) gives 4.8 + 3 × 2.4 = 12 µs. The lesson teaches both models (`#delay-models`,
  `#trap-block-vs-gate`) and the solution states which one the key uses. `needsReview` cleared through
  `src/gate/fixes/cse-2015-2.json`; erratum B note added.
- `cse-2006-cs-36` (`needsReview` stays): the 2006 scan (`GATE2006.pdf` p. 5, Q36) prints the four
  options as an illegible bitmap; only their shape (7 product terms each in A and B) can be read. The
  key A is proved by expanding $c_3 = G_2 + P_2G_1 + P_2P_1G_0$ with $P_i = a_i + b_i$ and $c = 0$ (the
  "carry generate" of the 3-bit block); the script checks A equals it and that B, C, D as transcribed
  do not. The option text cannot be confirmed from the scan.
- `cse-2016-2-cs-7`: the transport-delay simulation of an 8-bit ripple adder (from all-zero inputs)
  finds B = −1 the unique slowest operand with A = 1 (the carry generated at bit 0 travels through all
  seven propagate stages and out).
- `cse-2002-b-56-a`: simulating the three register stages clock by clock gives 0000, 0000, 1011, 1000,
  1010, 1011, 0000, 0000, the bank's key.
- `cse-2004-cs-62`: under its convention (every gate one unit, complements free, XOR built as AND-OR):
  P/G 2, carries 2, sum XOR 2 = 6. `cse-2007-cs-35`: $C_1 … C_4$ two-level need 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10 ANDs and
  4 ORs.
- `cse-1996-a-46`: exhaustive simulation: inputs 0–11 pass through, 12–15 become 1 0000 … 1 0011,
  i.e. two base-12 digits.
- `cse-2003-cs-46`: K, C₀ ∈ {00, 01, 10, 11} give A + B, A + B + 1, A − B − 1, A − B; A + 1 needs B = 0
  (not a control line), so A + B and A − B but not A + 1.

## Lesson 1 · `dl-04-01-adders-ripple-carry` · Half/full adders, subtractors and ripple-carry adders

**Prerequisites:** dl-02-03 (XOR), dl-01-04 (signed numbers); number-level binary addition is
[Chapter 1, Lesson 9](lesson:code-arithmetic#binary-add). **Covered by sets:** A 48–59; B 129–131, 133;
C 279–288; D 400–401; E 408–409.

### Teaching sequence
1. **Radar** `#radar`; intuition `#adder-intuition` (adding one column by hand is a tiny truth table).
2. **Half adder** by the design procedure `#half-adder` (spec → table → maps → $S = A \oplus B$,
   $C = AB$); `:::derivation` `#ha-derivation`; animated `half-adder-design`.
3. **Full adder** `#full-adder`; `:::derivation` `#fa-equations` ($S = A \oplus B \oplus C_{in}$;
   $C_{out} = AB + BC_{in} + C_{in}A = AB + C_{in}(A \oplus B)$); theorem `#fa-carry-forms` + proof;
   from two half adders and an OR `#fa-from-ha`; theorem `#or-not-xor` (the two half-adder carries
   are never both 1, so the OR may be an XOR) + proof; animated `full-adder-from-has`.
4. **Gate counts** `#adder-gate-counts` (HA 2 gates; FA 5 gates from HAs; NAND-only HA 5 (minimum,
   by exhaustive search), FA 9; NOR-only HA 5, FA 9); `:::trap` `#trap-ha-count`.
5. **Subtractors** `#half-subtractor` ($D = x \oplus y$, $B = \bar x y$), `#full-subtractor`
   ($D = x \oplus y \oplus b$, $B_{out} = \bar x y + \bar x b + y b$); theorem `#fs-from-fa`
   (borrow = majority($\bar x$, y, b); a full subtractor is a full adder with $x$ inverted at the
   carry logic) + proof; `:::trap` `#trap-borrow-sign`.
6. **Ripple-carry adder** `#ripple-carry` ($n$ full adders, carry chained); counts `#ha-fa-count`
   (no carry in: 1 HA + (n − 1) FA; with carry in: n FA); animated `ripple-carry-wave`.
7. **Delay models** `#delay-models` (table: unit gate, SOP FA, block, carry/sum split); theorem
   `#ripple-delay` ($c_i$ ready at $3 + 2(i-1)$, last sum at $2n$, carry out $2n + 1$) + proof;
   `:::trap` `#trap-block-vs-gate` (2015: 19.2 vs 12).
8. **Worst-case operands** `#worst-case-carry` (a carry generated at bit 0 and propagated through
   every stage; $A = 1$, $B = -1$); data-dependent delay `#data-dependent` (aside, deep: average
   longest run ≈ log₂ n).
9. **Pipelined adders** `#pipelined-adder` (registers between full-adder stages; latency vs
   throughput; skewed inputs); animated `pipeline-adder` (2002).
10. **Live Lab** `#lab-race` (Adder Race, race tab). GATE questions, practice, summary.

### Worked examples (4 per concept)
- **Half and full adders:** HA outputs for 1 + 1 (easy); FA from a K-map (medium); FA from 2 HAs:
  the internal lines for 1 + 1 + 1 (GATE); edge case: replacing the OR by an XOR.
- **Subtractors:** half subtractor 0 − 1 (easy); full subtractor row 0 − 1 − 1 (medium); 4-bit
  ripple-borrow subtraction 5 − 7 (GATE); edge case: the borrow out read as the sign.
- **Delays:** 4-bit ripple, unit delays (easy); 16-bit with the SOP full adder (medium); the 2015
  numbers both ways (GATE); edge case: when the last sum, not the carry out, decides.
- **Worst case and pipelining:** operands that ripple all the way in 8 bits (easy); A = 1, which B
  (GATE 2016) (medium); the latency and throughput of a 4-stage pipelined adder (GATE); edge case:
  a pipelined adder whose inputs are not skewed.

### Diagrams and animations
- `half-adder-design` (animate): the 4-row table, the two one-cell maps, then the XOR and AND gates
  evaluating each row.
- `full-adder-from-has` (animate): A = 1, B = 1, C = 1 then other rows through HA1, HA2 and the OR,

- `ripple-carry-wave` (animate): a 4-bit ripple adder adding 0111 + 0001; the carry moves one stage

## Lesson 2 · `dl-04-02-carry-look-ahead` · Carry look-ahead adders

**Prerequisites:** dl-04-01. **Covered by sets:** A 59–60; B 131–133; C 286–287; D 401; E 408.

### Teaching sequence
1. **Radar**; intuition `#cla-intuition` (a relay race vs everyone reading the starting gun).
2. **Generate and propagate** `#generate-propagate` ($g_i = a_ib_i$, $p_i = a_i \oplus b_i$);
   theorem `#carry-recurrence` ($c_{i+1} = g_i + p_ic_i$) + proof; `#p-xor-or` (theorem: the
   recurrence also holds with $p_i = a_i + b_i$, but then $s_i \ne p_i \oplus c_i$) + proof;
   `:::trap` `#trap-p-or`.
3. **Unrolling** `#cla-expansion` ($c_4 = g_3 + p_3g_2 + p_3p_2g_1 + p_3p_2p_1g_0 + p_3p_2p_1p_0c_0$);
   `:::derivation` `#unroll-derivation`.
4. **The 4-bit CLA** `#cla-4bit` (p/g level, carry unit, sum XORs); delay `#cla-delay` (unit: 4;
   2004 convention: 6); gate count `#cla-gate-count` (n(n+1)/2 ANDs, n ORs; 2007: 10 and 4);
   fan-in `#fan-in` (the last AND has n + 1 inputs); animated `rca-vs-cla-timing`.
5. **Group G and P** `#group-gp` ($G = g_3 + p_3g_2 + p_3p_2g_1 + p_3p_2p_1g_0$, $P = p_3p_2p_1p_0$);
   theorem `#group-gp-theorem` (a block behaves like one big bit) + proof.
6. **Multi-level CLA** `#multi-level-cla` (16 bits = 4 blocks + a second-level unit); theorem
   `#cla-log-delay` ($\Theta(\log n)$ delay with bounded fan-in, $4\log_4 n$ unit levels for our
   construction) + proof; animated `cla-gp-tree`; 74181/74182 `#ic-74182` (1997);
   `:::trap` `#trap-constant-time` (two-level carries are "constant" only with unbounded fan-in).
7. **Hybrid adders** `#carry-select` (blocks computed for both carry ins, a 2:1 MUX picks; forward
   link to Chapter 5, Lesson 2); animated `carry-select`; `#carry-skip` (aside: skip a block when
   P = 1); `#ripple-block-cla` (CLA blocks rippled together); comparison table `#adder-compare`.
8. **Serial adder** `#serial-adder` (one FA + a carry flip-flop, n clocks).
9. **Live Lab** `#lab-scaling` (Adder Race, scaling tab).

### Worked examples (4 per concept)
- **G/P and the carry equations:** g and p of one bit pair (easy); c₃ by hand for given operands
  (medium); the 2006 carry generate as a sum of products (GATE); edge case: p as OR changes the sum.
- **CLA delay and cost:** 4-bit unit delay (easy); gate counts for 4 bits (medium, 2007); the 2004
  6-unit count (GATE); edge case: an 8-bit single-level CLA's fan-in.
- **Multi-level:** 16-bit G/P (easy); 16-bit delay vs ripple (medium); 64-bit levels and delay
  (GATE); edge case: 32 bits with fan-in 4 (a partly used top level).
- **Hybrid and serial adders:** carry-select 8 bits in two blocks (easy); its delay (medium);
  ripple of 4-bit CLA blocks for 16 bits (GATE); edge case: serial adder clocks and the carry
  flip-flop's reset.

## Lesson 4 · `dl-04-04-multipliers-booth` · Array multipliers (hardware)

## Chapter Live Lab: Adder Race (`:::lab{lab="adder-race"}`)

A new lab rendered by a lazily loaded component in `client/src/gate/notes/labs/` (`AdderRace.tsx`).
Logic lives in a pure, dependency-free module `adderLogic.ts` (netlist builders for the ripple-carry
and carry look-ahead adders with the adder–subtractor XOR row, static arrival times, the
transport-delay simulation and the flags), tested in `tests/gate-notes.test.js` against vectors that
`_checks/dl/l4_lab_vectors.py` writes and verifies with `arithlib`.

1. **Race** tab (`race`, lessons 1 and 3): width 4, 8 or 16; A and B as signed or unsigned numbers;
   add / subtract toggle; XOR delay 1 or 2 units. Both adders get the same input change (from the
   previous operands) and run side by side on one gate-delay clock (play, step, reset): carries light
   as they settle, a counter shows the elapsed gate delays and each adder's settling time, and the
   flag panel shows C, V (overflow), N and Z.
2. **Scaling** tab (`scaling`, lesson 2): worst-case delay against n (4 … 64) for ripple, CLA with
   fan-in 4 (multi-level) and carry-select, as a table and bars, using the same formulas the lesson
   proves.
