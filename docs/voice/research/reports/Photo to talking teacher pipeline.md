# Keep Ditto, add pre-rendered polish and consent

The best free, commercial-use-allowed pipeline for NexoraGuru is the one already started. **Ditto (Apache-2.0) with InsightFace replaced by MediaPipe** should be the single face engine for live tutor replies and for lesson narration. It is the only permissively licensed one-photo model with a published streaming mode: **385 ms to the first frame and a real-time factor of 0.895 on an A100** ([Ditto paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509)). Using one engine for both lanes also means the teacher looks the same in both. Three weaknesses have to be designed around, and none of them can be wished away. First, Ditto needs an NVIDIA GPU to be live. No free GPU may legally back a public website: Colab's free tier forbids web services, Kaggle bans "server farming", and ZeroGPU gives each visitor minutes a day ([Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html), [ZeroGPU docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-zerogpu.md)). So *live* photoreal replies for the public cannot be free at scale, and the site needs an honest fallback. Second, rivals measure Ditto's lip sync well below its own claims (Sync-C 4.09 against Live Avatar's 7.03) and its own user study rates its naturalness at only 48.7% ([Live Avatar paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04677v6), [Ditto paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509)). It therefore needs our own motion layer and our own quality gates, and flagship lessons should be bake-offs against **LongCat-Video-Avatar (MIT)** and **Kandinsky S2V (MIT)**, the two best open models in independent 2026 tables. Third, two files Ditto still loads (`landmark203.onnx` and `hubert.onnx`) have **unconfirmed licences**, and the photo has to go to a server, so India's IT Rules 2026 labelling and the DPDP Act apply in full. No model anywhere has a published Hindi lip-sync result, so Hindi quality is an open question that only our own test set can settle.

## The licence screen leaves Ditto for live use and two MIT giants for lessons

Most fast talking-head models fail the "commercial use, never guess" test, and the failures are rarely in the headline licence. **InsightFace's detector and landmark weights are "for non-commercial research purposes only"**, and since November 2025 InsightFace asks people to write in for a licence ([InsightFace README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deepinsight/insightface/master/README.md)). Almost every LivePortrait-family model loads those weights by default. LivePortrait's own LICENSE file tells commercial users to "remove and replace InsightFace's detection models" ([LivePortrait LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KwaiVGI/LivePortrait/main/LICENSE)). Ditto calls InsightFace only on the first frame, but it uses both the detector *and* the 106-point landmarker there ([source2info.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/antgroup/ditto-talkinghead/main/core/atomic_components/source2info.py)). Our wrapper (`scripts/teacher/talk/ditto_run.py`) already swaps both for MediaPipe Face Landmarker, which is Apache-2.0 ([MediaPipe LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/LICENSE)). That was the right first move.

The wrapper does not yet make Ditto fully clean, though. It still loads `landmark203.onnx`, which tracks the face after the first frame, and the HuBERT audio encoder. GitHub issues filed in 2026 ask about exactly these two: **#97 "License of landmark203 and HuBERT weights for commercial use"** and **#98 on the provenance of landmark203.onnx** ([#97](https://github.com/antgroup/ditto-talkinghead/issues/97), [#98](https://github.com/antgroup/ditto-talkinghead/issues/98)). Their bodies and any replies could not be read during this research. Ditto's Hugging Face repo carries an Apache-2.0 LICENSE file but no licence tag ([HF LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/digital-avatar/ditto-talkinghead/raw/main/LICENSE)). Until #97 and #98 are answered, treat these two files as unverified. The way out is clear. Every common HuBERT and wav2vec2 checkpoint is permissive (facebook/hubert-large-ls960-ft is Apache-2.0, TencentGameMate/chinese-hubert-base is MIT) ([HF hubert](https://huggingface.co/api/models/facebook/hubert-large-ls960-ft), [HF chinese-hubert](https://huggingface.co/api/models/TencentGameMate/chinese-hubert-base)). Tracking could be re-seeded from MediaPipe on every frame, as the first frame already is. Swapping the audio encoder would mean retraining Ditto's audio-to-motion part, which its released `train` branch makes possible.

Quality numbers in this field are self-reported and contradict each other, so read them carefully. On HDTF, Ditto reports **Sync-C 8.94**. IMTalker, a competitor, measured Ditto at **6.09** and itself at 7.71 ([IMTalker paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22167)). The Live Avatar benchmark put Ditto at 4.09, but also showed it was by far the fastest model there, at **21.8 fps against 0.16–0.64 fps** for the diffusion models ([Live Avatar paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04677v6)). Sync-C scales also differ between papers (about 8–9 on HDTF in some, about 1 in TalkBench), so compare numbers only within one table. The defensible reading is this. Ditto's lips are good, not best. Its motion is its real weakness: viewers ranked it behind Hallo2 and EchoMimic on naturalness, while it won visual quality (84.0%) and lip sync (80.7%) ([Ditto paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509)).

The diffusion and video-model family is where the quality ceiling sits now, and several of its members are licence-clean. In Kandinsky Lab's October 2026 comparison, **Kandinsky S2V Pro (distilled) ranked first (mean rank 2.30) and LongCat-Video-Avatar 1.5 second (2.90)** on EMTD, with LongCat posting the best Sync-C (8.41). HunyuanVideo-Avatar, Wan-S2V and InfiniteTalk followed ([Kandinsky S2V paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19290v1)). Both leaders are MIT for code and weights, and so are their base models ([LongCat README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/meituan-longcat/LongCat-Video/main/README.md), [HF LongCat-1.6](https://huggingface.co/api/models/meituan-longcat/LongCat-Video-Avatar-1.6), [kandinsky-s2v LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kandinskylab/kandinsky-s2v/main/LICENSE)). They also animate shoulders and hands, which suits a teacher. LongCat 1.6 came out on 15 November 2026 and has no independent test yet.

Several models are out. Sonic is CC BY-NC-SA. HunyuanPortrait is for academic use only, and V-Express checkpoints are for research only ([Sonic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jixiaozhong/Sonic/main/LICENSE), [HunyuanPortrait](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanPortrait/main/LICENSE), [V-Express](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tencent-ailab/V-Express/main/README.md)). Hallo2's high-resolution path is derived from CodeFormer, which is non-commercial ([CodeFormer LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sczhou/CodeFormer/master/LICENSE)). HunyuanVideo-Avatar is legal in India, but its licence forbids using the output outside a Territory that excludes the EU, UK and South Korea ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanVideo-Avatar/main/LICENSE)). A worldwide site cannot honour that, so it is out too.

The catch is cost. These models run at **0.16–0.6 fps on datacentre GPUs**. The fastest clean option, Kandinsky S2V Lite distilled, still needs **43 s of H100 time per 5-second chunk**, roughly 8.6 s of compute for each second of video ([Kandinsky S2V paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19290v1)). They belong in the narration lane and only for lessons that earn it.

## No free GPU may legally serve the public, so lanes split by where compute lives

A pixel-space photoreal teacher needs a GPU for every second it talks. The free options all fail for a public site. **Colab's free runtimes disallow "media serving, or other web service offerings"**, and Google banned deepfake projects from Colab in 2022 ([Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/01/2328459/)). Kaggle's acceptable-use policy bans "server farming" ([Kaggle AUP](https://kaggle.com/aup)). ZeroGPU gives a free account 5 minutes a day, and calls from a custom frontend fall into a 2-minute unauthenticated pool ([ZeroGPU docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-zerogpu.md), [huggingface_hub #3362](https://github.com/huggingface/huggingface_hub/issues/3362)). **Modal's $30 a month of free compute** is the only genuinely free GPU budget ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)). At an assumed T4 price, that is very roughly 50 GPU-hours a month: enough to batch-render lessons, not enough for an always-on tutor. Oracle Always Free has no GPU, and 2026 reports say its Arm allowance was halved to 2 OCPU / 12 GB ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/07/oracle-cloud-free-tier-limits/)). Oracle's own documentation still shows 4/24, so check the console.

The browser cannot pick up the slack for a photoreal warp model. No browser port of Ditto, LivePortrait or MuseTalk with a measured frame rate exists. The obstacle is the 5-D `grid_sample` warp, which needs a custom TensorRT plugin and a custom ONNX Runtime build even on servers ([FasterLivePortrait README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/warmshao/FasterLivePortrait/master/README.md)). The one photoreal-ish model that is ready to run on a device is **LAM**. It builds a Gaussian-splat head from one photo in about 1.4 s, its renderer is a WebGL library under MIT, and its makers report **110+ fps on a Xiaomi 14** ([LAM](https://github.com/aigc3d/LAM), [LAM_WebRender](https://github.com/aigc3d/LAM_WebRender)). ARKit blendshapes drive it, and a 30 fps blendshape stream costs about 94–375 KB a minute against 3.75–7.5 MB for 512² video (our own calculation, not a measured figure). But LAM ships a separate `LICENSE_WEIGHT` that nobody has read yet. Its expressions use FLAME topology, whose licence is also unread. The open-stack review describes its look as "a little gamey around hair and mouth interior", which is close to the 3D look the owner wants to avoid.

The architecture that follows has three lanes:

| Lane | What it does | Engine | Where it runs | Free? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Narration** (pre-rendered lessons) | Script → voice → video, once per lesson; served as MP4/HLS | Ditto offline (RTF 0.635 on A100); LongCat 1.6 or Kandinsky S2V Lite for flagship lessons | Modal credits, the model farm GPU, or the owner's machine as a batch job | Yes, within monthly credit |
| **Live** (Guru replies) | Reply text → streaming TTS → Ditto online → WebRTC | Ditto online config with TensorRT, MediaPipe swap | One GPU (model farm or Modal), reusing the site's Live/WebRTC stack | **Not free at scale**: GPU time per reply |
| **Live fallback** | When no GPU is free, or on weak phones | Pre-rendered idle, listening and thinking loops of the same person, plus the reply's audio and word-highlighted captions; later, optionally, a LAM head if its weight licence clears | Static files plus the existing CPU voice server | Yes |

Ditto adds about 0.4 s of first-frame delay on top of the TTS on an A100. On a T4 or a consumer card, the real-time factor may exceed 1.0, which has not been measured. Ditto's TensorRT engines are built for Ampere or newer, so a T4 (Turing) must convert from ONNX ([Ditto README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/antgroup/ditto-talkinghead/main/README.md)). One open bug report says the online config silently produces only about 5 frames through `inference.py` (#93) ([#93](https://github.com/antgroup/ditto-talkinghead/issues/93)). Our own wrapper runs on the CPU. **CPU-only is not practical for any of these models** at live speed, so the first measurement to take is our wrapper's CPU and T4 real-time factor. That number decides whether CPU-rendered narration is even an option for short clips. Open projects confirm the split. OpenAvatarChat (Apache-2.0) averages about 2.2 s per response with modular ASR, LLM, TTS and avatar parts, and LiveTalking needs at least 25 fps of inference and streaming per session ([OpenAvatarChat](https://github.com/HumanAIGC-Engineering/OpenAvatarChat), [LiveTalking](https://github.com/lipku/LiveTalking)).

## The photo intake must clean, check, consent and label before any frame exists

"Any photo" in practice means selfies with beauty filters, group shots, sunglasses, low light, turned heads and phone-compressed images. Ditto's crop simply returns nothing when it finds no face. Warp models show "stretching, tearing, or identity drift" on large poses and occlusions ([source2info.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/antgroup/ditto-talkinghead/main/core/atomic_components/source2info.py), [IMTalker paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22167)). So the intake has to be a pipeline of commercial-clean tools that fix what is safe to fix, and **ask for another photo** when fixing would mean inventing the person.

| Step | Tool | Licence | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find faces, 478 landmarks | MediaPipe Face Landmarker; YuNet fallback | Apache-2.0 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/MediaPipe-FaceLandmarker)); MIT ([YuNet](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/main/models/face_detection_yunet/LICENSE)) | Group photo → the person picks one face; refuse if any face looks under 18 |
| Parse hair, skin, clothes, accessories | MediaPipe multiclass selfie segmenter | Apache-2.0 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/MediaPipe-ImageSegmenter-Selfie-Multiclass)) | Its "others" class flags stickers, sunglasses and hats; avoid BiSeNet/SegFace weights (CelebAMask-HQ, unverified) |
| Cut out head and shoulders | BiRefNet / BiRefNet_HR-matting | MIT code and weights ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZhengPeng7/BiRefNet/main/LICENSE)) | Not RMBG-1.4/2.0, which are non-commercial ([RMBG-2.0](https://huggingface.co/api/models/briaai/RMBG-2.0)) |
| Upscale; restore the face lightly | Real-ESRGAN; GFPGAN v1.4 clean architecture or RestoreFormer++ at low blend | BSD-3 ([Real-ESRGAN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xinntao/Real-ESRGAN/master/LICENSE)); Apache-2.0 with NC third-party parts ([GFPGAN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TencentARC/GFPGAN/master/LICENSE)) | Never CodeFormer or 4x-UltraSharp; check identity before and after |
| Remove small occluders | LaMa (Big-LaMa) | Apache-2.0 ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/advimman/lama/main/LICENSE), [HF](https://huggingface.co/api/models/smartywu/big-lama)) | Anything over the eyes or mouth → ask for a new photo |
| Fix eyes, mouth, small head turns | LivePortrait retargeting (MediaPipe-seeded) | MIT ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/KwaiVGI/LivePortrait)) | Fix yaw/pitch under about 15°; reject over about 30° |
| Light and colour | Gray-world white balance, CLAHE, gamma | No licence | Check face-skin colour shift (ΔE) so darker skin is never lightened |
| Beauty-filter check | Heuristics: skin high-frequency energy, landmark ratios, accessory mask, EXIF | Ours | No licensed detector exists; ask for an unfiltered photo |

Each tool has a licence caveat. GFPGAN's LICENSE bundles NVIDIA's StyleGAN2 terms ("non-commercially… research or evaluation purposes only") for its CUDA ops and DFDNet code under CC BY-NC-SA ([GFPGAN LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TencentARC/GFPGAN/master/LICENSE)). Only the clean architecture without the custom ops is usable, and its FFHQ training data has unsettled terms. Restorers invent skin detail, which is why the identity gate runs before and after them. For "lightly stylised" output, the notes found no tool with a checked licence that keeps identity. Treat it as a later research item, not part of this plan.

Identity checks need a face embedder that is not InsightFace's ArcFace. Two are clean. **dlib's face-recognition ResNet was released into the public domain**, and **OpenCV-zoo SFace is Apache-2.0** ([dlib-models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/davisking/dlib-models/master/README.md), [SFace](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/main/models/face_recognition_sface/README.md)). The training data behind both has unverified terms, and dlib's bias on Indian and darker faces has not been measured, so use both together and calibrate them on Indian faces. **EdgeFace weights are CC BY-NC-SA** and MiVOLO v2 is CC BY-NC, so both are out ([EdgeFace](https://huggingface.co/Idiap/EdgeFace-Base), [MiVOLO](https://huggingface.co/api/models/iitolstykh/mivolo_v2)). For the age gate, **dlib's `dnn_age_predictor_v1` is CC0** ([dlib-models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/davisking/dlib-models/master/README.md)). Its accuracy on Indian faces is unknown, so refuse below an estimated 21 and send borderline cases to a person.

The law makes the intake non-optional. The **IT Amendment Rules 2026 (G.S.R. 120(E), in force 20 February 2026)** cover any intermediary offering "AI image/video generation" or "voice cloning tools" ([MeitY FAQ](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/065b6deb585441b5ccdf8be42502a49c.pdf)). A photoreal teacher is synthetically generated information (SGI) by design. The exclusion for "educational/training materials" covers documents and presentations and probably does not reach a realistic face; that is our reading, not a ruling. The rules require several things:

- a label "ensuring prominent visibility" on video
- a "prominently prefixed audio disclosure" on audio
- permanent metadata or provenance "including a unique identifier", to the extent technically feasible
- no "remove watermark" or "export without metadata" feature
- takedown within **3 hours** of an order
- a ban on SGI that misrepresents a person's "identity, voice, conduct, action or statement"

The draft's fixed 10% label size was dropped for "prominent" ([Rödl & Partner](https://www.roedl.it/en-gb/it/insights/pages/tech-data-bites/2-26/beyond-draft-indias-amended-it-rules-synthetically-generated-information.aspx)). These quotes come from MeitY's FAQ; the gazette text itself was not opened. The clean tools exist: **Meta Video Seal (MIT, models included)** for an invisible watermark that survives WhatsApp stripping metadata, and **c2pa-rs / c2pa-python (MIT/Apache)** for the manifest ([Video Seal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/videoseal/main/LICENSE), [c2pa-rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/contentauth/c2pa-rs/main/LICENSE-MIT)).

Consent should copy the industry pattern. HeyGen and Synthesia both require a **live consent video that must match the photo**, and Synthesia adds a spoken passcode ([HeyGen consent docs](https://developers.heygen.com/docs/avatar-consent), [Synthesia help](https://help.synthesia.io/en/articles/13846500-photo-avatars-content-moderation)). Ours would work like this: a webcam clip in the browser in which the person reads a random passcode and a fixed English or Hindi consent sentence. The server checks the passcode by speech recognition, matches the face to the photo with dlib plus SFace, checks liveness from MediaPipe blinks or a head turn, and estimates age. The site keeps the consent record and discards the embeddings.

This pipeline uploads the photo, which the earlier, device-only Teacher Maker design deliberately avoided. So the DPDP duties of notice, consent, purpose limits and erasure apply directly. **DPDP Rule 10 requires verifiable parental consent for anyone under 18**, and its obligations apply from about 13 May 2027 ([PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf), [DSCI brief](https://www.dsci.in/files/content/knowledge-centre/2025/Insight-Brief-on-DPDP-Rules.pdf)). Refusing minors outright is simpler than verifying parents. The strongest single control against impersonation is a content rule: **the teacher only ever speaks site-generated lesson scripts and Guru replies, never free text a user types.**

## Motion comes from a rule layer on Ditto's motion space, not from a better model

No permissively licensed model was shown to make natural listening and thinking behaviour. KDTalker has the best measured head-motion diversity (0.760, above real video's 0.639), but it is CC BY-NC ([KDTalker paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12963)). Learning to Listen and EMOTE ship no licence file, and SelfTalk is CC BY-NC ([SelfTalk](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/psyai-net/SelfTalk_release/main/LICENSE)). The training sets most of these models use (CelebV-HQ, CelebV-Text) forbid commercial use "of derived data", and HDTF's CC BY covers only the annotations of YouTube clips that other people own ([CelebV-HQ](https://github.com/CelebV-HQ/CelebV-HQ), [HDTF](https://github.com/MRzzm/HDTF)). Ditto inherits that grey zone through its weights, and the owner should know it.

The practical answer is a behaviour layer driven by speech research. It works on Ditto's LivePortrait-style motion keypoints, which already allow separate eye and lip retargeting. Ditto also computes eye attributes with MediaPipe and has an emotion condition whose removal drops Sync-C from about 8 to 6.31 ([Ditto paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19509)). Editing keypoints between the motion model and the renderer is our inference, untested. The targets are well supported:

- **Blinks.** People blink about **26 times a minute in conversation against about 17 at rest**, and fewer during demanding visual tasks ([Brych et al. 2021](https://doaj.org/article/360238ef0e524934a56d072810e322ee)). Listeners place blinks in "feedback slots" at the ends of the speaker's phrases ([Hömke, Holler & Levinson](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291193/)).
- **Gaze.** Speakers look away while planning a hard answer ([Kendrick & Holler 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/112376/1/Kendrick_Holler_2017_.pdf)). That gives a natural "thinking" glance of about a second before a long Guru reply.
- **Brows.** Brow raises match pitch rises about **71%** of the time, and about 38% happen while not speaking ([Cavé et al. 1996](https://www.isca-archive.org/icslp_1996/cave96_icslp.html)). Most pitch accents have none, so raising on every stressed word would look robotic ([Carignan et al. 2020](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10152455/1/Carignan_etal_ISSP_2020.pdf)).
- **Head.** Head motion tracks pitch more than loudness, and natural head motion makes speech easier to understand ([Munhall et al. 2004](https://www.queensu.ca/psychology/sites/psycwww/files/uploaded_files/Faculty/Kevin%20Munhall/Munhall_Psyc_Sci.pdf)).
- **Nods.** Listener nods come after a stretch of low pitch followed by a pause, at about 4 a minute in casual English ([Ward & Tsukahara 2000](https://www.cs.utep.edu/nigel/abstracts/jprag00.html)).

Breathing rate, blink duration and the Indian head-waggle as a backchannel had no source in this research. Treat those values as tunable guesses.

Listening and thinking need footage, not only rules. The safe source is **our own recordings**: 30–60 minutes of consenting staff talking and listening in Hindi, English and Hinglish, under a signed release covering likeness, voice, model training and commercial use. That footage serves three purposes: it can drive the motion layer, it can fine-tune Ditto's audio-to-motion part, and it gives the gates a real-person baseline. Per teacher, render a short set of idle, listening and thinking loops once, so the live fallback and the gaps between replies never freeze.

| Kokoro-82M | Apache-2.0 | 4 Hindi voices graded "C" ([VOICES.md](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md)) | None | Already live on the site; the fallback voice |

Several voice options are excluded. **XTTS-v2** uses the Coqui Public Model License, and **VibeVoice** is "limited to research purpose use" and English/Chinese only ([XTTS-v2](https://huggingface.co/coqui/XTTS-v2), [VibeVoice](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/VibeVoice-1.5B)). **Fish Audio** allows commercial use only below USD 500,000 revenue and forbids offering cloning as a hosted service ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/main/LICENSE)). Narrating the teacher's own lines would be allowed, but the cap means it is not "free forever". **Sarvam Bulbul** is a paid API with ₹1,000 of starting credits ([pricing](https://sarvam.ai/api-pricing)).

For live replies, Veena claims under 80 ms latency, but on an H100. svara says it suits CPUs through GGUF exports, but publishes no speed. So the live lane should start with Kokoro, or with svara or Veena on the same GPU as Ditto, and adopt the clone only once a measured first-audio time fits the latency budget.

Ditto takes audio directly and needs no visemes. Phoneme timings still matter for three things: caption word highlighting, the p/b/m closure gate, and any blendshape fallback. None of the TTS models above was confirmed to output them. Use **ctc-forced-aligner or WhisperX (both BSD-2)** with commercial-clean acoustic models: `ai4bharat/indicwav2vec-hindi` (Apache-2.0) for Devanagari and wav2vec2 or Whisper large-v3 (Apache-2.0) for English ([ctc-forced-aligner](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MahmoudAshraf97/ctc-forced-aligner/main/LICENSE), [indicwav2vec-hindi](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indicwav2vec-hindi)). Never use ctc-forced-aligner's default MMS model, which is **CC BY-NC 4.0** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/MahmoudAshraf/mms-300m-1130-forced-aligner)). For Hinglish, split by script or by language before aligning. For Hindi visemes, the ACL W13-3904 table maps Hindi phonemes onto the standard 22-viseme set with about 70% viseme-class accuracy from 100 native speakers ([ACL W13-3904](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/W13-3904.pdf)). Aspirates share their plain consonant's shape, retroflexes use the alveolar shape, and व needs a lip-rounding blend.

Hindi lip sync itself is the biggest unknown. **No talking-head model has a published Hindi result.** Hallo's authors say its audio "must be in English" ([Hallo README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fudan-generative-vision/hallo/main/README.md)). Models built on English-only wav2vec2-base-960h (IMTalker, OmniAvatar, FantasyTalking, FLOAT) are the weakest bet. Whisper-based LongCat and Qwen2.5-Omni-based Kandinsky are the likeliest to handle Hindi phonemes ([LongCat README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/meituan-longcat/LongCat-Video/main/README.md), [Kandinsky S2V paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19290v1)). The ER-NeRF authors found HuBERT "performs better for more languages" ([ER-NeRF README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Fictionarry/ER-NeRF/main/README.md)), which is mildly good news for Ditto, but which HuBERT Ditto ships is unknown. This is a hypothesis to test with a Hindi and Hinglish clip set, not a conclusion.

## Automatic gates and blind A/B decide "perfect", not papers

Since every vendor ranks itself first, the release gate has to be ours. Every rendered video passes automatic checks, each built from a tool with a checked licence:

- **Lip sync.** SyncNet's LSE-C and LSE-D. The `syncnet_python` code is MIT ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/joonson/syncnet_python/master/LICENSE.md)); its weights' training-data terms were not checked. Gate on a baseline measured on real footage of the same person in the same language.
- **p/b/m closures.** The lips must be closed within ±1 frame of every aligned bilabial, using the aligner above and MediaPipe lip distance.
- **Identity.** The median dlib and SFace distance between the cleaned photo and sampled frames, plus a check on every 1-second window to catch drift. InfiniteTalk's authors warn of colour shifts beyond about a minute from a single image ([InfiniteTalk README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MeiGen-AI/InfiniteTalk/main/README.md)), so long lessons should restart from the photo at sentence boundaries.
- **Blinks.** MediaPipe eye-aspect ratio; flag no blink in over 10 s or more than about 40 a minute.
- **Jitter and decay.** Head-pose-stabilised landmark velocity, background flicker inside the BiRefNet mask, Laplacian sharpness on the mouth crop, and per-frame face-detection confidence.

FID and FVD compare sets, not single videos, so use them only to compare engine versions on a fixed test set.

The human gate is a **blind, randomised side-by-side**. The same script is rendered by engine A and engine B and shown left and right in random order without labels. Raters answer "more natural?", "better lip sync?" and "same person?". Choices are logged in the database, and the win rate gets a binomial confidence interval. A comparison stops only when the interval excludes 50%, which typically takes 30 or more paired judgements; that is a rule of thumb to tune. This harness is what lets the site choose honestly between Ditto, IMTalker, LongCat 1.6, Kandinsky S2V Lite and a MuseTalk mouth pass on *our* Hindi clips.

The main risks are four. The first is legal: the Ditto sub-model provenance, dataset-derived weights across the whole field, IT Rules wording read only from the FAQ, and DPDP duties now that photos are uploaded. The second is cost: live photoreal replies need paid or credit-limited GPU time, and a promise of an "always-on live teacher for free" would be false. The third is quality: Ditto's middling naturalness, unproven Hindi lips, and identity drift on long renders. The fourth is misuse: impersonation, minors and lookalikes. The content rule and consent matching reduce it; they do not remove it.

The build should go in phases, each finished before the next:

- **Phase 0, pin facts (one week).** Read Ditto #84/#97/#98 and ask the maintainers. Read LAM's `LICENSE_WEIGHT`, the gazette text of G.S.R. 120(E) and DPDP Rule 10, Google's MediaPipe model-card PDFs, and the IndicF5 card. Measure our wrapper's real-time factor on the CPU, a T4 and the model farm GPU. Record every decision with dated copies in the repo.
- **Phase 1, intake and safety.** The photo pipeline, consent video, age and identity gates, the burned-in label, the audio prefix, C2PA plus Video Seal, the report button and a 3-hour takedown queue in Admin. Nothing public ships before this.
- **Phase 2, narration lane on Ditto.** Batch rendering, the rule motion layer, recorded idle/listen/think loops, the automatic gates, and a Hindi/Hinglish Sync-C test set.
- **Phase 3, voices.** The aligner and Hindi viseme table for captions and gates, then the Chatterbox consented clone, with svara or Veena LoRA as the bake-off rival.
- **Phase 4, live lane.** Ditto online on one GPU over the site's WebRTC stack, Guru replies streamed sentence by sentence, and the loop fallback when no GPU is free.
- **Phase 5, quality bake-off.** LongCat 1.6, Kandinsky S2V Lite, IMTalker and a MuseTalk mouth pass against Ditto in blind A/B on our clips. Keep the winner for flagship lessons.
- **Phase 6, our own motion.** Fine-tune Ditto's audio-to-motion part on consented Indian recordings using its `train` branch, under `docs/voice/TRAINING.md`. That removes most of the dataset grey zone and teaches it Hinglish rhythm.

## Conclusion

The research moves the hard problem away from the model. A clean one-photo engine exists, and the team has already made its key licence fix. The deciding questions are elsewhere: where GPU seconds come from, whether two small ONNX files are clean, and whether the site can prove consent and label every frame. Choosing Ditto for both lanes is less about peak quality than about **one consistent face**, a model fast enough to iterate on, and a motion space we can edit. Quality upgrades (LongCat, Kandinsky, a mouth pass) then become swappable renderers, judged by our gates rather than by vendors' tables.

Two things should be said plainly to the owner. Live photoreal replies for every student cannot be free, so the free product is excellent pre-rendered lessons plus a live mode that degrades gracefully to loops and captions. And "perfect Hindi lip sync" is today an untested hypothesis for every model on the market. The first Hindi clip set the team scores will be more informative than all the published papers combined.
